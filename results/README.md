# HAAR Experiment --- Questionnaire and Results

## Self-Application of HAAR to the HAAR Scientific Article

This directory documents the first self-application experiment in which
the **HAAR framework was applied to the scientific article describing
HAAR itself**.

The experiment was conducted interactively in **ChatGPT**. Two files
were uploaded into the same conversation:

1.  the **HAAR scientific article**, used as the work to be analyzed;
2.  the **HAAR Framework specification**, used as the operational
    assessment procedure.

ChatGPT was then asked to **analyze the article according to the
uploaded HAAR framework**.

No questionnaire was prepared manually in advance. The questionnaire was
generated by ChatGPT from the scientific reasoning reconstructed from
the article.

The practical workflow was:

``` text
Upload HAAR scientific article
             +
Upload HAAR Framework
             ↓
Ask ChatGPT:
"Analyze the article according to the HAAR Framework"
             ↓
Reconstruct the scientific reasoning represented in the article
             ↓
Identify milestones, critical transitions and hidden transitions
             ↓
Generate a work-specific HAAR questionnaire
             ↓
Conduct the questionnaire interactively
             ↓
Record the respondent's answers
             ↓
Apply transformational questions and delayed cross-checks
             ↓
Construct the Reasoning Evidence Graph
             ↓
Generate the HAAR Evidence Profile
```

This experiment therefore tested whether a general-purpose LLM could
read the HAAR specification as an **operational framework**, apply it to
an uploaded scientific work, generate a personalized assessment, and
produce structured reasoning evidence.

> **HAAR → EVIDENCE**\
> **HUMAN → DECISION**

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Purpose of the Experiment

The purpose was **not** to determine whether the article was written by
a human or generated by an AI system.

The experiment tested a different proposition:

> Can HAAR reconstruct the scientific reasoning represented in a
> submitted work and assess whether the respondent can reconstruct,
> justify, challenge, transform, and extend its critical reasoning
> transitions?

The article itself was therefore treated as:

``` text
SOURCE_WORK
```

and the uploaded HAAR specification as:

``` text
ASSESSMENT_SPECIFICATION
```

The experiment is deliberately self-referential:

``` text
HAAR Framework
      ↓
HAAR Article
      ↓
HAAR Assessment
```

For that reason, it should be interpreted as an **initial
proof-of-concept and self-application experiment**, not as independent
empirical validation of the framework.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Reconstructed Assessment Logic

At a high level, the reasoning represented in the article was
reconstructed as:

``` text
P → H → G_R → M → Q → A → G_E → R → HD
```

where:

-   `P` --- scientific problem;
-   `H` --- hypothesis;
-   `G_R` --- Semantic Reasoning Network;
-   `M` --- milestones and critical transitions;
-   `Q` --- personalized questionnaire;
-   `A` --- respondent answers;
-   `G_E` --- Reasoning Evidence Graph;
-   `R` --- HAAR Evidence Profile;
-   `HD` --- Human Decision.

The questionnaire therefore focused on the reasoning architecture of the
article rather than on stylistic properties of the text.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Questionnaire Design

ChatGPT generated **20 questions** using several HAAR reasoning
operations:

``` text
WHY
WHY-NOT
ALTERNATIVE
COUNTERFACTUAL
REMOVE
CONTRADICT
LIMIT
TRANSFER
EXTEND
REPRODUCE
DELAYED CROSS-CHECK
FINAL INTEGRATION
```

Each question contained five answer alternatives (`A`--`E`).

The questionnaire tested, among other things:

-   Text Origin → Reasoning Ownership;
-   Scientific Text → Semantic Reasoning Network;
-   milestone criticality;
-   work-specific questionnaire generation;
-   Anti-Coaching;
-   Delayed Cross-Check;
-   Evidence Graph interpretation;
-   `S_HAAR ≠ P(authorship)`;
-   structural perturbation;
-   contradictory evidence;
-   transfer to dissertation-level assessment;
-   extension of research;
-   HAAR Evidence → Human Decision.

During the active assessment, answers were recorded sequentially. The
respondent was not given explanatory teaching feedback after every item,
so that later responses would not simply reproduce reasoning supplied by
the assessment system.

## HAAR Question 1 --- WHY / Hidden Transition

from text origin to reasoning ownership. Which rationale best
corresponds to the scientific reasoning underlying this transition?

-   **A.** Reasoning ownership is easier to calculate automatically than
    stylistic characteristics, so HAAR essentially replaces a complex
    classifier with a simpler one.
-   **B.** The involvement of an LLM in producing a text does not, by
    itself, determine the independence of the underlying scientific
    reasoning; therefore, academic assessment can more informatively
    examine whether a person can reconstruct, justify, and transform the
    critical reasoning transitions represented in the work.
-   **C.** Determining text origin is fundamentally impossible, so all
    authorship-attribution methods should be replaced by oral
    examination.
-   **D.** Reasoning ownership and text authorship are equivalent: if a
    respondent can correctly explain the reasoning of a work, this
    directly proves that the respondent created the text.
-   **E.** The primary purpose of the shift is to calculate SHAAR, which
    can then be interpreted as the probability that the respondent
    authored the work. **Recorded answer: `B`**

## HAAR Question 2 --- ALTERNATIVE / Milestone

relationships.

Which explanation best justifies this design choice?

-   **A.** A conventional entity graph cannot contain weighted edges,

    with the transitions among them; this makes it possible to assess

    merely their knowledge of entities mentioned in the text.
-   **C.** A reasoning network guarantees that every edge represents a
    strict causal relationship and therefore makes the scientific
    argument mathematically provable.
-   **D.** Entity graphs are suitable only for natural-language
    processing, whereas reasoning networks can be used exclusively for
    academic assessment.
-   **E.** The main purpose of GR is to reduce the entire scientific
    work to a small set of keywords from which authorship can be
    automatically determined. **Recorded answer: `B`**

4.  Counterfactual and transformational reasoning

The strongest part of the assessment was not factual recognition but the
responses to structural perturbations.

In Question 15, removal of method (M) from

\[
H`\rightarrow `{=tex}M`\rightarrow `{=tex}E`\rightarrow `{=tex}O`\rightarrow `{=tex}I`\rightarrow `{=tex}R

consideration of alternative interpretations, and only then

\[ `\text{REMOVE}`{=tex} `\rightarrow `{=tex}`\text{CONTRADICT}`{=tex}

7.  An important result for improving HAAR itself

The experiment exposed a weakness that I think is worth incorporating
into the framework specification: answer-position leakage.

The questionnaire generator currently specifies plausible distractors
and one identifiable target, and the validator checks ambiguity and
distractor quality. HAAR_Framework_EN_v1.1 But this experiment shows
that validation should also control the distribution of target
positions.

I would add a rule such as:

\[ `\text{TargetPosition}`{=tex}(q_i)
`\not`{=tex}`\approx `{=tex}`\text{predictable pattern}`{=tex}. \]

More formally, for a questionnaire (Q={q_1,`\ldots`{=tex},q_n}), if
(p_j) is the empirical frequency with which position (j) contains the
target answer, then the generator should constrain positional imbalance,
for example:

\[ `\max`{=tex}\_j p_j-`\min`{=tex}\_j
p_j`\leq`{=tex}`\varepsilon`{=tex}. \]

For five-option questions, the desired distribution should be
approximately

\[
p_A`\approx `{=tex}p_B`\approx `{=tex}p_C`\approx `{=tex}p_D`\approx `{=tex}p_E`\approx0.20`{=tex}.
\]

This does not mean mechanically cycling A→B→C→D→E, because that itself
creates another predictable pattern. Target positions should instead be
randomized or balanced subject to an anti-pattern constraint.

I would call this new HAAR requirement the Answer-Position Neutrality
Principle. It is a concrete methodological improvement that emerged
directly from applying HAAR to its own article.

## Supported Reasoning Transitions

### 1. Text Origin → Reasoning Ownership

Questions 1 and 8 revisited the distinction between the origin of

The answers remained consistent across the two formulations.

### 2. Scientific Text → Semantic Reasoning Network

Question 2 tested why HAAR reconstructs a reasoning network rather than
merely an entity graph.

The selected answer preserved the role of hypotheses, assumptions,

### 3. Milestone → Criticality

Question 7 tested why structural centrality alone is insufficient.

The selected answer recognized that a structurally central transition
may be routine, whereas a less central transition may be scientifically
decisive because of conclusion dependency, non-triviality, uncertainty,

### 4. Milestone → Personalized Questionnaire

Questions 3, 11, and 13 tested the rationale for work-specific and

The responses consistently favored reasoning operations over simple
textual reproduction.

### 5. Assessment → Anti-Coaching

Question 6 tested what happens if immediate correctness feedback and

The selected answer identified contamination of subsequent evidence: the
system could begin teaching the reasoning it is supposed to assess.

### 6. Initial Response → Delayed Cross-Check

Questions 3, 8, and 9 supported the principle that an important

### 7. Local Responses → Evidence Graph

Question 18 preserved the fundamental separation:

The final judgment may require provenance, institutional context,

# Experimental Finding: Answer-Position Leakage

An important methodological result emerged **during the assessment
itself**.

After Question 13, the respondent noticed that intended target answers
were disproportionately concentrated in position **B**.

The response-position counts over the complete recorded trajectory were:

``` text
A = 1
B = 10
C = 5
D = 1
E = 2
```

More importantly, the concentration was especially visible in the first
part of the questionnaire.

This created an unintended secondary information path:

``` text
Scientific reasoning
      ↓
Correct answer
```

but also potentially:

``` text
Answer position
      ↓
Predictable cue
      ↓
Correct answer
```

The second path is methodologically undesirable.

The observation therefore concerned the **quality of the generated
assessment instrument**, not the respondent's reasoning ability.

After the regularity was identified, target positions in Questions
14--20 were deliberately varied:

``` text
Q14 = D
Q15 = A
Q16 = E
Q17 = C
Q18 = E
Q19 = B
Q20 = C
```

# From the Experiment to HAAR v1.2

The answer-position observation directly motivated a methodological
extension of HAAR:

## Answer-Position Neutrality Principle

Let `p_j` be the empirical frequency with which position `j` contains
the target answer.

A simple positional imbalance measure is:

``` text
Δ_pos = max_j(p_j) − min_j(p_j)
```

with a desired constraint:

``` text
Δ_pos ≤ ε_pos
```

For five-option questions, a sufficiently large questionnaire should
tend toward:

``` text
p_A ≈ p_B ≈ p_C ≈ p_D ≈ p_E ≈ 0.20
```

However, deterministic cycling is also prohibited:

``` text
A → B → C → D → E → A → B → ...
```

because a perfectly balanced deterministic sequence is still
predictable.

The requirement is therefore:

``` text
POSITIONAL BALANCE
        +
POSITIONAL UNPREDICTABILITY
```

or, in compact form:

``` text
QUESTION SEMANTICS ≠ ANSWER POSITION
```

This experimental observation became one of the concrete methodological
improvements incorporated into **HAAR Framework v1.2**.

# Limitations of the Experiment

This experiment should not be interpreted as independent empirical
validation of HAAR.

First, it is a **self-application experiment**: the framework was
applied to an article describing the same framework.

Second, the questionnaire used multiple-choice alternatives. Carefully
designed multiple-choice items can test transformations, alternatives,
counterfactuals, and structural perturbations, but they provide less
evidence of spontaneous reasoning reconstruction than free-response
questions, oral defense, graph reconstruction, or research-provenance
tasks.

Third, the answer-position regularity in the first part of the
questionnaire weakens the evidential value of treating the raw `20/20`
result as an independent quantitative performance measure.

Accordingly, the most useful result of this experiment is not the raw
score. It is the demonstration of the complete operational workflow and
the identification of a concrete weakness that led to an improved
framework specification.

# Reproduction Procedure

The experiment can be reproduced with a general-purpose LLM.

``` text
1. Start a new ChatGPT conversation.

2. Upload the scientific work to be assessed.

3. Upload the HAAR Framework specification.

4. Ask ChatGPT to:
   "Analyze the submitted scientific work according to the HAAR Framework
   and conduct an interactive HAAR assessment."

5. Allow the system to reconstruct:
   - the Semantic Reasoning Network;
   - milestones;
   - critical transitions;
   - hidden transitions.

6. Allow the system to generate a work-specific questionnaire.

7. Answer questions sequentially.

8. Do not request correctness explanations during active assessment.

9. Use functionally different delayed checks for important transitions.

10. After completion, request:
    - Reasoning Evidence Graph;
    - supported / uncertain / contradictory transitions;
    - Delayed Cross-Check analysis;
    - HAAR Evidence Profile;
    - methodological observations.

11. Interpret the result as evidence of reasoning command,
    not as an automatic determination of historical authorship.
```

For HAAR v1.2, the questionnaire should additionally undergo:

``` text
OPTION PERMUTATION
        ↓
ANSWER-POSITION AUDIT
        ↓
POSITIONAL BALANCE + UNPREDICTABILITY
```

before or during deployment.
